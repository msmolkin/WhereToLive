# Panama City, Panama - Living Cost and Lifestyle Research

## 1. Food Prices

### Monthly Grocery Costs
- **Single person**: $200-$400 per month
- **Typical shopping trip**: $93.58 covers a wide variety of items including frozen foods, premium coffee, cat food, eggs, cheese, fresh produce, and household items
- **Budget-conscious single person**: Rarely spends more than $95 per supermarket trip

### Common Grocery Items
Based on actual shopping receipts from Rey supermarket:
- Premium Panamanian coffee (Janson brand)
- Eggs (dozen)
- Fresh produce: green beans, tomatoes, potatoes, broccoli, watercress, lettuce, basil, limes
- Imported cheese (scamorza): $8.60
- Mushrooms (including portabellas)
- Frozen spinach
- Meat alternatives (Gardein imitation chicken)

### Eating Out Costs
- **Traditional restaurants**: 3 tacos for $11, chicken quesadilla for $8.50
- **Beverages**: Diet Coke $2.50
- **Fish dishes**: Additional $3 for preparation (grilled or breaded)
- **Ceviche**: Available at various price points from market stalls to upscale restaurants
- **Mercado de Mariscos**: Best value for fresh ceviche and seafood

### Supermarket Comparison
Major chains include:
- **Riba Smith**: Higher-end supermarket
- **Super 99**: Mid-range option
- **El Rey**: Popular chain with loyalty program offering 2% cashback
- **Local markets**: Significantly cheaper for fresh produce (Mercado de Mariscos for seafood, Mercado San Felipe Neri for general produce)

**Money-saving tip**: Shopping at local markets and choosing local products over imported items can reduce grocery costs by 30-50%.

## 2. Commuting Costs & Transportation

### Public Transportation
**Panama Metro**:
- Line 1: $0.35 per trip
- Line 2: $0.50 per trip
- Combined trip (transfer between lines): $0.85
- Students: $0.17 per trip (with personalized card)
- Metro/Metrobus card: $2.00-$4.00 (required for all trips)
- Card can store up to $50.00 in fares

**Metrobus**:
- Inner city routes: $0.25
- Corridor routes: $1.25
- No cash accepted - card required

### Car Ownership
- **Gasoline**: Approximately $4.00 per gallon ($1.10 per liter)
- **Car insurance**: $58.32 per month (significantly cheaper than US)
- **Maintenance**: Much lower than US costs
- **Monthly gas expense**: As low as $30 for compact cars
- **Parking**: Generally affordable in most areas

### Taxi and Ride-Hailing
- Taxis don't have meters - fares are set by authorities
- Airport to Casco Viejo/Banking District: Varies but reasonable
- Licensed taxis recommended for safety
- Multiple passengers may share rides (common practice)

### Transportation Habits
- Metro system is expanding with good coverage in main areas
- Many residents combine Metro, Metrobus, and taxis
- Car ownership is practical despite traffic congestion
- Peak hour traffic can be severe on main corridors
- Public transportation doesn't cover all areas comprehensively

## 3. Housing Costs & Rental Process

### Property Purchase Prices (per square meter)
- **Casco Viejo**: $4,000-$4,300/m² (premium historic area)
- **El Cangrejo**: Starting at $2,720/m²
- **Punta Pacifica**: $4,300+/m² (luxury oceanfront)
- **Costa del Este**: $2,800-$3,200/m²
- **San Francisco**: $2,720/m²

### Monthly Rental Costs
- **El Cangrejo**: $800-$1,500/month (depending on size/quality)
- **Furnished apartments**: 10% premium over unfurnished
- **Example**: Unfurnished condo at $1,000/month = $1,100/month furnished

### Mortgage and Purchase Costs
- Mortgage interest rates: Competitive (specific rates not provided)
- Property purchase allowed for foreigners with same rights as nationals
- Required fees include transfer tax and legal fees

### Rental Process for Foreigners
**Required Documentation**:
- Valid passport (minimum 6 months validity)
- Proof of income (pay stubs, bank statements, tax returns)
- Bank reference letter (valid for 30 days)
- Proof of address (can be hotel letter if newly arrived)
- Criminal record certificate (apostilled) may be required

**Key Points**:
- No permanent residency required to rent
- Documents must be translated to Spanish by authorized translator
- Real estate agents commonly used
- Typical lease terms: 1 year
- Security deposit: Usually 1-2 months
- Working through licensed real estate agents recommended to avoid fraud

### Utilities
- **Electricity**: $100-$200/month (up to $300 with heavy AC use)
- **Water**: Often included in rent, or about $8-9/month
- **Internet**: $35-$60/month for 60+ Mbps
- **Total utilities**: $150-$200/month average

**Air conditioning note**: Split systems in each room are standard; central AC is rare. Heavy AC use significantly increases electricity costs.

## 4. Political Climate & Safety

### Current Political Climate (2025)
- State of emergency declared in Bocas del Toro Province (May 27, 2025)
- Nationwide demonstrations occurring since late April 2025
- Generally stable democracy with strong ties to US
- Dollarized economy provides stability

### Safety Statistics
- Panama crime index: 45.15 (compared to US: 47.81, France: 51.99)
- 2024 saw 4.4% increase in homicides nationally
- Popular tourist areas like Casco Viejo saw 3% reduction in violent crime
- US State Department rating: Level 2 - Exercise increased caution

### Safe vs. Unsafe Neighborhoods
**Areas to Avoid**:
- El Chorrillo, Curundu, Santa Ana, San Miguelito
- Areas near Colombian border
- Darién Province
- Mosquito Gulf region

**Safe Areas**:
- Upscale neighborhoods with regular patrols
- San Francisco (walkable and safe)
- Tourist areas and business districts
- Casco Viejo (particularly LGBT+ friendly)

### Crime Types
- Most common: Petty theft, pickpocketing
- Property crime in tourist areas
- Violent crime concentrated in specific high-risk neighborhoods
- Gang activity limited to certain areas

## 5. Visa Requirements & Residency Options

### Tourist Entry (US Citizens)
- **No visa required** for stays up to 180 days
- Requirements: 
  - Passport with 3+ months validity
  - Round-trip ticket
  - Proof of $500+ in funds

### Residency Options

**Friendly Nations Visa**:
- Available to US citizens
- Requirements: $200,000 real estate investment OR establish business/employment
- Grants 2-year temporary residence, then permanent residence
- Path to citizenship after 5 years

**Pensionado (Retiree) Visa**:
- Minimum $1,000/month lifetime pension
- Add $250/month per dependent
- Immediate permanent residence
- Includes discounts on medical, transport, entertainment

**Investment Visas**:
- $300,000 in real estate
- $160,000 in business
- $80,000 in reforestation project

**Work Permits**:
- Separate from residency visa
- Requires sponsorship and Ministry of Labor approval
- Must obtain residency first, then apply for work permit

### Legal Requirements
- All applications must be filed through Panamanian immigration lawyer
- Documents must be apostilled
- Spanish translations required

## 6. Healthcare System

### Hospital Infrastructure
Major facilities in Panama City:
- Hospital Punta Pacifica (Johns Hopkins affiliated)
- Hospital Nacional
- Hospital Santo Tomás
- Ciudad de Salud (opened January 2024)

### Healthcare Costs
**Doctor Visits**:
- General practitioner: $20
- Specialist: $60-65 (with pensionado discount)
- Public system: $1.50

**Procedures**:
- Blood tests: $15
- X-rays: $18
- Complete lab workup: $42 (with 25% pensionado discount)
- Dental cleaning: $50
- Dental implant: $600

### Insurance Options
**Local Plans**:
- Basic: $30-$200/month
- Age-based pricing examples:
  - 30-39: $51.98/month
  - 50-59: $94.30/month
  - 70-79: $146.25/month
- Couple coverage: $138-$190/month

**International Plans**:
- Age 64: $2,500/year with $500 deductible
- 65+: $4,500+/year
- Budget option: $1,400/year

**Additional Options**:
- MiniMed Expat Membership: $20/month or $220/year
- Access to 14 clinics and hospital in Panama City

## 7. Language & Cultural Considerations

### English Accessibility
- **Business sector**: Widely spoken
- **Medical community**: Many English-speaking doctors
- **Tourist areas**: Good English availability
- **Daily life**: Spanish essential for everyday tasks
- **By neighborhood**:
  - Boquete: More English than Spanish spoken
  - Panama City: Mixed, depends on area
  - Coronado: English widely spoken

### Expatriate Communities
**Established Communities**:
- **Boquete**: Largest expat community (North Americans/Europeans)
- **Panama City**: Mix of working and retired expats
- **Coronado**: 5,000+ population with large expat presence
- **Bocas del Toro**: Significant expat community

**Support Infrastructure**:
- Regular expat meetup groups
- English-language websites and directories
- Community events and activities
- Starlink internet available for remote areas

### Banking & Financial Services
- US dollar is official currency (no exchange rate concerns)
- Developed banking sector
- International business environment
- Tax advantages for foreign income

## Quality of Life Summary

### Pros:
- Significantly lower cost of living (36.3% less than US)
- Excellent private healthcare at fraction of US costs
- Strong expat communities and support
- No currency exchange issues (uses USD)
- Modern infrastructure in main areas
- Direct flights to US cities
- Pensionado program offers substantial discounts

### Cons:
- Spanish necessary for daily life outside expat areas
- High humidity and AC costs
- Traffic congestion in Panama City
- Some areas have safety concerns
- Limited public transport coverage to all areas
- Bureaucracy can be challenging without lawyer assistance

### Overall Assessment
Panama City offers an attractive option for US citizens seeking a lower cost of living with modern amenities. The combination of dollar stability, established expat communities, quality healthcare, and various residency options makes it feasible for Americans to relocate safely and legally. The city is particularly well-suited for retirees with the Pensionado program and those who can work remotely or invest in local business/real estate.
